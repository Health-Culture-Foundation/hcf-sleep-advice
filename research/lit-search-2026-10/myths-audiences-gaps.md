# Myths, audiences and gaps: literature search summary

Area: `myths-audiences-gaps`. Searched 2026-10-21. 57 findings from 43 sources in `myths-audiences-gaps.json`.

## What was searched

I searched PubMed through NCBI E-utilities and checked open access on OpenAlex. Topics:

- Sleep myths: Robbins 2019 and the adolescent follow-up, myth prevalence surveys, and YouTube, TikTok and AI quality studies.
- Trends: mouth taping, orthosomnia and sleep trackers, and consumer sleep technology position statements.
- Audiences: school start times, bedtime routines, sleep duration by age, infant safe sleep and bed-sharing, sleep position in pregnancy and stillbirth, menopause, shift workers, older adults (naps, hypnotics, light), college students, insomnia with mental disorders, and siesta.
- Gaps: Irish 2015, Baranwal 2023, sleep hygiene education meta-analysis, nicotine and vaping, pets, bed partners and snoring, paradoxical intention, and snoozing.

For most sources I read only the abstract. I read the full-text tables in PMC for three papers:

- Robbins 2019: all 20 myths, plus the 4 statements that were not rated as myths.
- Robbins 2022: the 10 adolescent myths.
- Shriane 2023: all 18 shift-worker guidelines.

The search for "sleepmaxxing" returned no PubMed results. A search for sleep divorce or separate beds found nothing usable.

## Strongest findings per concept

| Concept | Finding | Suggested support |
|---|---|---|
| myth-snoring-is-harmless | Experts rated it false (4.25/5). A small study found partners' sleep efficiency rose from 74% to 87% when the snorer used CPAP (Beninati 1999). | 4 |
| myth-alcohol-helps-sleep | Experts rated it false (4.13/5) (Robbins 2019). | 4 |
| myth-tv-helps-you-sleep | Experts rated it false (3.50/5): supported, but less firmly. | 3 |
| myth-weekend-catch-up | Experts did *not* classify weekend sleeping-in as a myth (falseness 2.50). In UK accelerometer data (n = 73,513), weekend catch-up sleep was not linked to death or heart disease, which is consistent with "doesn't make up for it". | 2–3 |
| get-enough-sleep-for-you | "Many adults need only 5 hours or less" had the highest public health rating of any myth (4.63). NSF 2015 gives ranges by age: teens 8–10 h, adults 7–9 h, older adults 7–8 h. | 4 |
| get-up-if-you-cant-sleep | "Stay in bed and keep trying" was rated a myth (4.63). Shriane guideline 18 agrees. | 4 |
| keep-naps-short-and-early | A meta-analysis of 44 cohorts (1.86M people) found risk for naps of 30 minutes or more and none under 30 minutes. In older adults, short naps may help cognition. | 3 |
| cbt-for-insomnia | Strong across audiences: menopause (RCT, 70–84% reached the no-insomnia range), mental disorders (22 RCTs), and college students (digital). Sleep hygiene education alone does worse than CBT-I (42 RCTs). | 4–5 |
| adjust-to-night-shift-work | Shriane 2023 Delphi: 18 tailored guidelines. A meta-analysis found a moderate effect, g = 0.59. | 4 |

| screens-before-bed | NSF consensus: screens impair sleep in children and teens. The panel did not reach consensus for adults. | 4 for kids, lower for adults |
| have-a-wind-down-routine | Bedtime routines in young children (Mindell 2018 review). | 4 for children |

## Advice that should not be given to everyone

- **Shift workers** need different advice for these concepts: keep-naps-short-and-early, keep-a-regular-sleep-schedule, get-up-at-the-same-time, get-morning-daylight and caffeine-cutoff.
  - Shriane 2023 recommends naps: 15–20 minutes to stay alert, or 90 minutes to pay back sleep debt.
  - It recommends sunglasses on the drive home after a night shift.
  - It says caffeine before and during a shift can help.
- **People with insomnia** shouldn't use naps to make up lost sleep; experts rate that a myth (Robbins 2019). This is the opposite of the shift-worker advice.
- **Older adults:** "keep naps short" is better than "avoid naps." Sleeping pills give small benefits (+25.2 minutes of sleep) with much higher rates of cognitive side effects (4.78×) (Glass 2005). Bright light for older adults with insomnia did no better than dim light in one RCT.
- **Children and teens** need more sleep than the adult 7–9 hours. Melatonin "because it's natural" is a common parental myth (66% of parents believe it). Do not give melatonin-for-jet-lag-or-shifts to minors.
- **Infants:** adult co-sleeping or bed-sharing advice must never apply to babies. AAP 2022 recommends back sleeping, a firm and flat surface, and sharing the room but not the bed. In a meta-analysis, bed-sharing had an OR of 2.89 for SIDS, and 10.37 for babies under 12 weeks. Bed-sharing is normal in many cultures, so this needs careful wording.
- **Pregnancy:** from 28 weeks, going to sleep on the back is linked to stillbirth (aOR 2.63). The **right side is as safe as the left** (aOR 1.04), so "sleep on your left side" is overstated. A prospective study up to 30 weeks found no link.
- **Menopause, depression, PTSD:** CBT-I works. Don't withhold it because of a mental-health condition.

## Disagreements with current concepts (need an editor's decision)

1. **rest-even-if-awake** conflicts with Robbins 2019. Experts rated "lying in bed with your eyes closed is almost as good as sleeping" strongly false (4.63). Reword the concept so it doesn't suggest that rest replaces sleep, or drop it.
2. **stay-in-bed-and-breathe** conflicts with get-up-if-you-cant-sleep. The expert myth rating (4.63) and the shift-worker consensus both say to get up.
3. **no-hard-workouts-right-before-bed** and the second half of myth-exercise-right-before-bed: experts rated "exercising within 4 hours of bedtime will disturb your sleep" a myth (3.25), and Shriane 2023 says night exercise is fine for most people. Consider "evening exercise is fine for most people; leave time to wind down."
4. **Snoozing:** experts in 2019 called snoozing a myth (3.75), but a 2024 lab study found 30 minutes of snoozing cost about 6 minutes of sleep with no clear harm. I recommend not adding an anti-snooze rule.
5. **Pets:** experts did not rate "sleeping with a pet improves sleep" as a myth (2.63). The data show no harm in teens, and slightly lower sleep efficiency when a dog is on the bed rather than in the room.

## New advice found (no matching concept)

- **Nicotine and vaping:** this is the only Irish 2015 hygiene item with no concept. Shriane 2023 says to avoid nicotine within 6 hours of bed, and vaping is linked to short sleep (OR 1.38). Suggested line: "Avoid smoking or vaping in the hours before bed; if you quit, sleep may be rough for a few weeks before it improves." (support 2–3)
- **Paradoxical intention:** "If trying hard to sleep keeps you awake, stop trying." A meta-analysis of 10 trials found it helps. (support 3)
- **Sleep trackers:** "Use one lightly; it can't diagnose; stop if it makes you anxious." The AASM 2018 position statement backs this, and the orthosomnia concern comes from case reports. A small trial found trackers didn't increase worry. (support 2–4)
- **Sleep advice online:** "Be wary of sleep advice from videos and influencers." 66.7% of popular YouTube sleep videos had commercial bias, and only 15.7% of TikTok insomnia videos had high educational value. (support 3)
- **Dozing off easily:** "Falling asleep anywhere, any time is a sign you need more sleep." Experts rated the opposite belief a myth (4.75). (support 3)
- **Later school start times for teens:** a 2026 meta-analysis found about 69 more minutes of sleep. The Cochrane 2017 review rated the evidence very low quality. This is policy advice that parents and schools can act on. (support 3)
- **Infant safe sleep** (AAP 2022). (support 5)
- **Side-sleeping from 28 weeks of pregnancy**, either side. (support 4)
- **Older adults and sleeping pills.** (support 4)
- **Pets:** "Keep a pet off the bed if it wakes you." (support 2)
- **Bed partners:** "Get a partner's snoring checked." Bed-sharing itself doesn't harm sleep. (support 2)

Baranwal 2023 produced no new items: every recommendation it lists already has a concept.

## Gaps (searched, found little good evidence)

- No peer-reviewed study of "sleepmaxxing", and no evidence base for separate beds or "sleep divorce".
- No good cross-cultural studies of the siesta beyond observational napping cohorts. Most of those come from China, where napping is the norm.
- The AASM 2022 melatonin-in-children health advisory (PMID 36239049) has no abstract and its text wasn't in PMC XML, so it wasn't read or cited.
- The NSF 2026 consumer sleep technology position statement has a non-specific abstract, is closed access, and several authors are industry employees. I left it out of the findings.
- The Cochrane review on CBT-I in perimenopause is only a protocol so far.
