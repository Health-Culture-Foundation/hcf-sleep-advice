# Other conditions: RLS, narcolepsy, circadian, pediatric insomnia and OSA

46 rows in `other-conditions.json`: 38 guideline, 6 pathway, 2 coverage. Every PMID was checked with E-utilities esummary on 2026-10-21. Most rows were read from the abstract only. Full text was read for AASM circadian 2015, ATS 2024, China pediatric OSA 2020 and the NHS England sodium oxybate policy.

## Main differences

1. **RLS: the US moved off dopamine agonists, but Europe's formal guideline hasn't.**
   - AASM 2025 (PMID 39324694) *suggests against standard use* of pramipexole, ropinirole, rotigotine and levodopa. It strongly recommends gabapentin, pregabalin, gabapentin enacarbil and IV ferric carboxymaltose.
   - The only pan-European guideline is EFNS/ENS/ESRS 2012 (PMID 22937989), which gives dopamine agonists **Level A**. It has not been updated, and no EAN RLS guideline exists.
   - The World Sleep Society endorsed AASM 2025 only partly (PMID 40834711). The dopamine agonist recommendations were "endorsed with caveats" because of "regional disagreement". Dipyridamole was not supported.
   - Italy (RLS-Italia 2026, PMID 42891733) has adopted the US position: α2δ ligands preferred, dopamine agonists "restricted to selected patients".
   - Iron thresholds are higher than for ordinary anaemia: treat if ferritin ≤ 75 ng/mL or TSAT < 20%, and give IV iron only if ferritin is 75-100.
2. **Narcolepsy: the guidelines agree, but payers set a different order.**
   - AASM 2021 and EAN/ESRS/EU-NN 2021 both rate modafinil, pitolisant, sodium oxybate and solriamfetol STRONG for sleepiness.
   - NHS England funds sodium oxybate in children only as **third-line**, after methylphenidate, then dexamfetamine, lisdexamfetamine, modafinil or atomoxetine, for at least 3 months.
   - NICE TA758 allows solriamfetol only after modafinil plus dexamfetamine or methylphenidate. The committee said pitolisant and sodium oxybate depend on individual funding requests.
   - The EMA (2010) restricted modafinil to narcolepsy and removed OSA, shift-work and idiopathic hypersomnia. AASM still strongly recommends modafinil for idiopathic hypersomnia.
   - In the US, oxybates can only be dispensed through the FDA REMS (certified pharmacies, 30-day first fill).
3. **Melatonin in children: three different access models.**
   - **US:** melatonin is a supplement anyone can buy. The AASM 2022 advisory tells parents to see a clinician first and to buy USP-verified products.
   - **EU, Australia, Japan:** only licensed prescription products. EU and Australia: Slenyto for ASD, neurogenetic disorders, and ADHD aged 6-17. Japan: Melatobel for neurodevelopmental disorders.
   - **Denmark (2023):** the most restrictive position. Melatonin "should never be the first choice" in otherwise healthy children, and use for insomnia caused by other disorders should be started by specialists.
4. **Pediatric OSA testing ranges from "never without PSG" to "no sleep study needed".**
   - **China 2020:** cannot diagnose without PSG, and the treatment threshold is OAHI > 1/h.
   - **US:** AAP 2012 wants PSG. AAO-HNS 2019 requires PSG only for children under 2 or in high-risk groups.
   - **Canada 2026:** first to accept home testing, for healthy children over 5 when PSG is effectively unavailable.
   - **France 2023:** a sleep study is not mandatory if there are at least two night-time symptoms.
   - **Montelukast:** China gives it a 1B recommendation for mild to moderate OSA. ATS 2024 gives it only a conditional, very-low-certainty suggestion.
5. **Circadian disorders:** the only formal guideline found is AASM 2015, which is stale. All its recommendations are weak, except one strong against hypnotics in older people with dementia. Tasimelteon was not graded.

## Surprises
- Most guidelines in this area are stale (more than 5 years old): AAP 2012, ERS 2016/2017, AASM circadian 2015, AASM pediatric behavioral 2006, EFNS RLS 2012, and both narcolepsy guidelines (Sep 2021, just over 5 years).
- The AASM RLS panel is closely linked to the IRLSSG and RLS Foundation, and the chair consulted for the maker of ferric carboxymaltose. The AASM hypersomnolence chair had to step out of the oxybate, pitolisant and solriamfetol votes.
- The Japanese melatonin trial behind Melatobel was uncontrolled and run with the manufacturer (Nobelpharma).

## Absences (findings)
- No Japanese, Korean or Chinese RLS guideline was found in PubMed.
- A Chinese narcolepsy guideline exists ("中国发作性睡病诊断与治疗指南 (2022版)", Zhonghua Shen Jing Ke Za Zhi 2022;55(5):406-420, per citing papers) but is not in PubMed. Not read.
- No Japanese narcolepsy guideline was found.
- No circadian guideline outside the US was found apart from BAP 2019 (PMID 31271339), which is closed access and was not read.
- The AASM has no pediatric insomnia guideline newer than 2006.
