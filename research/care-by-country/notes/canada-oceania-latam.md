# Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Latin America: findings (slug: canada-oceania-latam)

43 rows in `canada-oceania-latam.json`, covering adult chronic insomnia and OSA. Layers: guideline 29, coverage 12, pathway 2. By country: Canada 15, Australia 12, Brazil 10, NZ 2, Argentina 2, Mexico 1, Chile 1 (absence row).

## Main differences
- **CBT-I first everywhere, but who pays differs.** Australia is the only country here with a national subsidised route: insomnia counts as an eligible condition under Medicare Better Access, which gives up to 10 psychology sessions a year on a GP Mental Health Treatment Plan (2026 joint statement). In Canada I found no public CBT-I coverage: psychologists are outside OHIP, and Ontario Structured Psychotherapy officially covers anxiety and depression only. NZ and Brazil: no coverage found.
- **Orexin antagonists (DORAs): approved but not paid for.** Canada approved lemborexant in 2020 and daridorexant in 2023, but CDA-AMC said "do not reimburse" for both: daridorexant in 2025 (upheld on reconsideration), lemborexant twice (2023 and Jan 2026). Australia has suvorexant and lemborexant, neither on the PBS. Brazil's 2023 guideline recommends all three DORAs at evidence Level 1-2, even though none is confirmed as sold in Brazil: daridorexant was filed with ANVISA in 2025, with launch expected in 2027.
- **Brazil is the most drug-friendly.** It recommends zolpidem (Level 1, max 4 weeks), eszopiclone, low-dose doxepin 3-6 mg and trazodone for staying asleep. Its panel declared many pharma COIs (Eurofarma, EMS, Apsen, Aché and others). EMS is daridorexant's Latin American partner. The same year ANVISA tightened zolpidem: from Aug 2024 every dose needs the blue controlled prescription (Notificação de Receita B), because of misuse.
- **Australia 2026 joint statement (ASA, SHF, RACGP, APS, PSA, APNA).** Benzodiazepines and Z-drugs for up to 2-4 weeks, melatonin up to 13 weeks, DORAs "generally only" for chronic insomnia. It advises against antihistamines, antidepressants, antipsychotics, cannabinoids and complementary medicines. This contrasts with Brazil and with Ontario's CEP tool, which call doxepin the safest choice in older adults.
- **Melatonin rules vary widely:**
  - Canada: over-the-counter Natural Health Product, adults only.
  - Australia: pharmacist-only for 55+ (2 mg MR), prescription otherwise.
  - NZ: since 10 Oct 2025, pharmacy-only for all adults 18+ (30 days for insomnia).
  - Brazil: food supplement capped at 0.21 mg/day, with no sleep claims allowed.
  - Mexico IMSS (older adults) and the Brazil guideline: not recommended for adult insomnia.
- **OSA home testing.** Accepted with conditions in Canada (CTS 2011: moderate-high pretest probability, PSG back-up), Brazil (SBPT 2022, high-risk patients) and Argentina (AAMR 2019, Evidence A). Australia sets the gate through Medicare: a GP refers, and a sleep or respiratory physician approves a home study based on STOP-Bang >=3 / OSA50 >=5 / Berlin high risk plus ESS >=8. That physician does not have to see the patient.
- **Who pays for CPAP:**
  - Ontario ADP pays 75% (100% on social assistance). The patient must be assessed by a physician at an ADP-registered sleep clinic.
  - Australia: Medicare pays for studies, not devices. Free state loans are means-tested. Queensland requires a concession card, a sleep physician, and a 2-month trial averaging >=4 h/night, which is an adherence rule like US Medicare's.
  - Brazil SUS has no OSA protocol, CPAP has not been assessed by CONITEC, and it is on no dispensing list.
- **Wait-time standard:** only Canada sets one: severe or safety-critical cases within 4 weeks, everyone within 6 months (CTS 2011).

## Surprises
- Canada's adult OSA guideline is 15 years old (CTS 2011). The only new CTS/CSS sleep guideline (2026) is for children.
- The Alberta TOP insomnia guideline (2015) is effectively retired. The AMA site now links only to a questionnaire and to Ontario's CEP tool, which was last updated in 2017.
- MBS STOP-Bang cut-off is **3**, not the 4 that many secondary sources still quote. It changed in March 2021.
- Daridorexant does not appear at all in the 2026 Australian medicines table. I did not confirm its TGA status.
- NZ melatonin was deregulated in Oct 2025, so it is now looser than Australia.
