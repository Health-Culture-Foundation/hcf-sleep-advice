# Europe: adult chronic insomnia and OSA guidelines (slug: europe)

67 rows in `europe.json`: 47 guideline, 10 pathway, 10 coverage. Covers ESRS 2023 and 2017, ERS OSA documents, EU Directive 2014/85/EU, Germany, France, Netherlands, Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Finland (blocked), and one row each for Italy, Spain and Switzerland. The UK is left to another agent.

## Main differences

**How long you can take sleeping pills (BZ/Z-drugs)**
- Denmark: usually 1-2 weeks at first, and anything over 2 weeks counts as long-term (Sundhedsstyrelsen 2019).
- Norway: "usually up to two weeks, at most four weeks".
- Netherlands: no hypnotics for persistent insomnia at all. A one-off, short course is allowed only for short-term insomnia with a clear cause, "for example at most 2-3 weeks", with 5-10 tablets per prescription.
- France: 4 weeks maximum including the taper (triazolam 2 weeks), set by regulation (ANSM). Zolpidem needs a secure prescription form since April 2017.
- Germany and ESRS: 4 weeks or less.
- Italy (expert consensus): up to 4 weeks, but eszopiclone up to 6 months.

**Orexin antagonists (daridorexant)**
- ESRS 2023: up to 3 months (grade A), longer in some cases.
- German S3 2025: no recommendation for long-term use (secondary source).
- German payer (G-BA, Aug 2023): daridorexant is exempt from the 4-week reimbursement cap when insomnia has lasted at least 3 months with considerable daytime impact.
- Italy: "at least 3 months and up to 1 year".
- Netherlands: not mentioned.

**Low-dose sedating antidepressants**
- ESRS allows them, short-term (grade B).
- NHG (NL) does not recommend amitriptyline or mirtazapine, and gives a strong recommendation against quetiapine.
- HAS 2006 says they are unproven.

**Melatonin**
- ESRS: fast-release melatonin is not recommended (A). Prolonged-release melatonin "may be effective" up to 3 months in people over 55 (B).
- NHG: do not prescribe melatonin to adults (no exception for PR).
- Supplement rules differ by country (ANSES 2018 survey): banned in Denmark and Czechia; 0.3 mg/day or more counts as a medicine in Germany and Belgium; up to 1 mg/day allowed in Spain and Italy; under 2 mg per dose allowed in France.

**Antihistamines**
- ESRS recommends against them (A).
- France's HAS 2006 still allows doxylamine and other H1 drugs in adults, and says herbs may be used "as placebo". The French guideline is 20 years old.

**Digital CBT-I**
- Germany is the only country here that pays for it: three permanently listed DiGA (HelloBetter Schlafen, somnio, somnovia), manufacturer prices about 225-241 euro, no co-payment.
- ESRS rates digital CBT-I as equal to in-person (A).
- NHG names guided online CBT (I-Sleep) as an option within its "lightest suitable format" stepped care.

**OSA diagnosis and CPAP**
- Germany: the payer rule (G-BA MVV-RL) is a fixed step sequence: history, then exam, then home polygraphy of at least 6 h read by a sleep physician, then lab PSG only if polygraphy cannot decide. CPAP is started under PSG, usually over 2 nights, with a polygraphy check at 6 months. No hours-per-night rule appears.
- Germany: an oral appliance is reimbursed only after CPAP fails, which conflicts with the guideline's first-line framing (DGSM/DGZS 2024 position paper, secondary source).
- France: to be reimbursed, CPAP needs at least 3 symptoms plus AHI of 30 or more (or 15-29 with severe sleepiness on PSG, or with a serious comorbidity), and prior authorisation. Prescribers must consider an oral appliance first in the 15-29 band.
- France adherence rule: at least 112 h per 28 days (4 h/night). 56-111 h is tolerated for a while; under 56 h counts as poor use.
- France, HAS proposals (March 2026): GP or nurse can renew from the 3rd prescription, a stop rule for persistent use under 56 h, and a 40-hour training minimum for prescribers.
- Netherlands: the GP refers suspected OSA to a pulmonologist, ENT surgeon or neurologist and does no testing.

**ERS non-CPAP guideline (2021)**
- All recommendations are conditional, with very low to low quality evidence.
- Hypoglossal nerve stimulation: salvage only, AHI under 50 and BMI under 32. The German S3 2020 window is wider: AHI 15-65 and BMI up to 35.
- No mention of GLP-1 drugs or tirzepatide.

**EU Directive 2014/85/EU**
- Moderate OSA = AHI 15-29 and severe = AHI 30 or more, both with excessive daytime sleepiness.
- Drivers with suspected OSA are referred before getting or renewing a licence.
- Review at least every 3 years for group 1 (cars) and every year for group 2 (lorries, buses).

## Surprises
- **NHG is far stricter than ESRS.** It has no hypnotics for chronic insomnia, no antidepressants and no melatonin, and it counts insomnia as "persistent" from 3 weeks.
- **France's insomnia guidance is from 2006 and its clinical OSA guideline is from 2010 (SPLF).** In practice, French OSA care is shaped by payer rules (LPPR), not clinical guidelines.
- **German guidance and German payment rules disagree twice.** The 2025 guideline gives no recommendation for long-term daridorexant, but the payer covers it beyond 4 weeks. The guideline treats oral appliances as a first-line option, but the payer covers them only after CPAP fails.
- **The ESRS 2023 paper contradicts itself on PR melatonin.** The abstract says "≥ 55 years" and Table 16 says "> 55 years".
- **The European deprescribing consensus has industry ties (Palagini 2025).** It favours daridorexant and PR melatonin, and its authors report Idorsia ties. Idorsia makes daridorexant.
- **The AWMF register reserves its guidelines against text-and-data mining (§44b UrhG) and against AI use.** So I did not mine the German PDFs, and the German 2025 insomnia rows rest on a pharmacy-press summary (PTAheute).
