Short answer

Sleep tips help, but they don't treat insomnia.The American Academy of Sleep Medicine (2021) advises clinicians not to use "sleep hygiene" alone for insomnia that has lasted months, and reviews find it works less well than CBT-I, a short course of talking and habit therapy that US and European guidelines name as the first treatment. Everyday tips are for keeping sleep healthy, not for fixing a sleep disorder. (Lead framing proposed, not yet agreed.)
Plain first, evidence underneath

Every item has three layers: one line you can act on, a short paragraph, then the studies.

Disagreements shown, not hidden

When trusted sources disagree, both positions are listed. Screens before bed is the clearest case: strong evidence for children and teens, much weaker for adults.

Who it fits

Items are tagged by audience (children, shift workers, pregnancy, older adults…) and note who a tip may not suit.

Where it comes from

  • Sleep Awareness Month wiki: 62 tips and 5 myths, with the wiki's write-ups and citations.
  • Health glossary sleep entries: 20 more tips from NHLBI and popular sleep experts (Michael Breus, Andrew Huberman), placed in the same categories.
  • Do One Thing habits from Sleep Literacy: 7 more tips (cannabis, finishing work earlier, planning social time earlier…).
  • October 2026 literature search: five research passes covering guidelines, body clock and light, mind and behavior, substances and the bedroom, and myths and gaps. They added 18 tips (infant safe sleep, nicotine, sleep trackers, later school start times…) and attached findings to most items. Every PubMed ID was re-checked.

Everyday advice for individuals is here. Two related pieces are not published yet: what works at the level of schools, workplaces and policy (the "What Works" intervention library, still private, one sleep study so far), and the full dataset with medical codes (held back until the code licences are checked).
